Describe papers in your browser

Your AI model reads twenty abstracts and writes, for each, the task the paper carries out, without its field's words. Nothing to install: the model runs in this page, or you use your own key. A unit takes a few minutes.

Your key, if you use one, stays in this browser and goes only to the service it belongs to. The abstracts come from OpenAlex; your descriptions are published under CC0 once you send them, with your GitHub name and the model's.

1Choose a model

The first unit waits for the download; later units start at once. Results name the model, so different volunteers' models can check each other.

The server must allow this page to call it. Ollama: start it with OLLAMA_ORIGINS=https://nicolas-maman.github.io ollama serve. LM Studio: turn on CORS in its server settings.

    3Send it

    What your papers resemble

    Once the unit is described, the papers from other fields that its descriptions are most like appear here: in this unit, and among the descriptions already published.

    Or start from your own paper

    Paste a paper's title and abstract, yours or any other. The model you chose above describes it without its field's words, and the page finds the papers already described, in other fields, that do the most similar thing. Nothing is sent anywhere but to your model, and nothing is published.

    How a result is checked

    1. You send it as a GitHub issue under your account, through a form the page opens for you.
    2. A job on the project checks it within minutes: no jargon, no description of one paper reused for another, and descriptions that match their own papers' abstracts. It answers on the issue.
    3. A unit is complete once two different models have described it, from anyone. Their descriptions are then compared, and papers from different fields that both models describe alike become pairs.

    Prefer a terminal, or want to run many units unattended? Use the command-line client. Something not working? The explorer's log shows every result the checking job turned away, and why.
