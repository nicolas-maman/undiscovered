undiscovered

Research in one field that another field could use, but has never cited.

In 1986 Don Swanson read two sets of papers that had never cited each other. One showed that dietary fish oil lowers blood viscosity, platelet aggregation and vascular reactivity. The other described Raynaud's syndrome, which involves all three. He proposed fish oil as a treatment, and a small double-blind trial in 1989 found that it helped patients with primary Raynaud's. He called this undiscovered public knowledge.

This project looks for such connections in every field of science, and publishes how often its predictions turn out to be right. Fields often describe the same problem in different words, so volunteers will lend their own computer and whichever AI model they prefer to rewrite papers without their field's jargon. The result will be an open index of papers described in plain terms, so that work on the same problem in different fields can be matched.

First result, October 2026: inconclusive. We froze the literature at the end of 2017 and ranked 28,114 pairs of topics from different domains that had barely cited each other; 305 of them started citing each other by 2023. The citation network around each pair ranked them about 14 times better than chance. Adding what the papers say, measured by the words they share, did not measurably improve on it, so under the plan written before the analysis the method is not taken further as it is.
