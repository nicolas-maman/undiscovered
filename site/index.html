undiscovered

Finding the research that should be talking to each other — and testing whether we're right.

In 1986 Don Swanson noticed that one body of papers said fish oil thins the blood, and another that Raynaud's syndrome involves thick, sluggish blood. Nobody had read both. He called it undiscovered public knowledge: findings already published, waiting in two literatures that never cite each other.

This project looks for those connections across all sciences, with an open network of volunteers who bring their own computer and any AI model they like — and it publishes how often it is right.

Status: building. The first milestone is a public backtest: freeze the literature at 2017, predict which distant fields start citing each other, check against 2018–2023, and compare with the published prior art. The result goes here either way.
