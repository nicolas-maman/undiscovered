undiscovered

Research in one field that another field could use, but has never cited.

In 1986 Don Swanson read two sets of papers that had never cited each other. One showed that dietary fish oil lowers blood viscosity, platelet aggregation and vascular reactivity. The other described Raynaud's syndrome, which involves all three. He proposed fish oil as a treatment, and a double-blind trial in 1989 found that it helped. He called this undiscovered public knowledge.

This project looks for such connections across all of science. Volunteers will be able to lend their own computer and whichever AI model they prefer, and the project publishes how often its predictions turn out to be right.

Status: building. The first milestone is a public backtest. We freeze the literature at the end of 2017, predict which unconnected fields start citing each other, check the prediction against 2018 to 2023, and compare it with the approach that did best in earlier work. The result will appear on the results page whether or not the method works.
