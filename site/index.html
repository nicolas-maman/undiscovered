Research in one field that another field could use, but has never cited.

Fields often solve the same problem under different names, and rarely read each other. This open project looks for those connections across all of science, and checks its predictions against what actually happens next.

Where the idea comes from

In 1986 the information scientist Don Swanson read two sets of papers that had never cited each other. One showed that fish oil lowers blood viscosity, platelet aggregation and vascular reactivity. The other described Raynaud's syndrome, which involves all three. He proposed fish oil as a treatment, and a small double-blind trial in 1989 found that it helped patients with primary Raynaud's. He called this undiscovered public knowledge: results already published, in literatures nobody reads together. Swanson worked by hand, in medicine. This project tries the same across every field.

How it works

  1. Map who cites whom. From OpenAlex, the open index of scholarly works: for each research topic, which other topics cite its most cited papers, and when. Pairs of topics far apart that have barely cited each other are the candidates.
  2. Describe papers without jargon. Volunteers' AI models rewrite each paper as the problem it solves, in plain words, so that a Kalman filter and data assimilation can be recognised as the same thing. Each paper is described by two volunteers, and only papers that two different models describe consistently are used.
  3. Check against what happens. Every test is planned in public before its outcomes are seen, predictions about the future are timestamped before the citations that judge them exist, and scores are published whichever way they come out.

Where it stands

First test, October 2026

Inconclusive. Frozen at the end of 2017, the citation network around 28,114 pairs of topics ranked the 305 that connected by 2023 about 14 times better than chance. Adding the words the papers share did not measurably improve on that. Read the results

Take part

Describe twenty papers, in your browser. Nothing to install: the model runs in the page itself, or you use your own key (Google, Groq and OpenRouter give free ones). A unit takes a few minutes, and the page shows what your papers resemble in other fields as soon as it is done. Start, or use the command-line client to run many units unattended.

Start from a paper of your own. Paste its title and abstract; a model describes it without its field's words and the page finds the papers in other fields that do the most similar thing. Nothing is published. Try it

Explore and check. The explorer has the map of the set, the pairs and the words that made them, a search by what papers do, and everything the checking job has accepted or turned away, and why.

Find a flaw. Criticism is worth most before the data is in. Tell us what could make a result wrong.
